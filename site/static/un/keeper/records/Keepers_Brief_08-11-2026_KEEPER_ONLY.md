## I — The opening: Fiona, out of the water

*Read-aloud, or near it. The evening starts with her, Landis, and Ali; Taxe arrives late.*

> The cold wakes you before the light does. You are floating — on your back, arms out, the way a leaf sits on a pond — and the sky above you is a color Midlandia never had a name for. There is salt in your mouth. There is nothing under your feet, and there has been nothing under your feet for longer than you can account for. You are bare as the day you were born, and the sea does not care, and — you find, surprising yourself — neither, much, do you. The palace would have cared. The palace is gone.
>
> Then the smell reaches you: salt, yes, but under it — tar, rope, coffee, a man's pipe — and the vision comes the way they always come, quick and sideways, gone before you can hold it: *a narrow table. Chairs with names on them. One chair empty, and someone laughing.* You blink the sea out of your eyes and the hull is already beside you — low, silver, patient, as though it had been standing there the whole time and the ocean had brought you to it rather than it to you.
>
> A rope ladder unrolls. A coat comes down over the rail first — good wool, worn at the cuffs — and behind it a warm, unhurried voice: *"Take your time. The water's already done the introducing."* Aboard the *Silver Surfer*, the man in the coat's twin writes something in a ledger without looking at it, and does not ask your name as though he needed it.
